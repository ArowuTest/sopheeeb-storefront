# Tree$hya’s Edit: customer-copy direction

## The job of the copy

Visitors usually arrive after seeing a TikTok. The website should make the next step obvious: play the original video, understand what they are looking at, and use a TikTok Shop link only when it is the exact verified product.

Copy must speak **to the visitor**, not narrate how the website was built or how its content is administered. It should feel clear, direct and genuinely useful—never like a developer explaining a missing feature.

## Voice

- **Customer first.** Lead with the visitor’s task: “Play the original video”, “Find the exact link”, or “Explore Fitness”.
- **Creator-led, not self-referential.** Use Tree$hya’s name and real content as the proof. Use first person only for an actual creator quote or a creator-authored note that adds trust.
- **Specific and factual.** Refer to what the clip shows: home workouts, try-ons, styling, beauty, wellness and everyday finds. Do not invent product benefits.
- **Clear about commerce.** A video can be played on-site. A Shop button only appears for an exact checked TikTok Shop destination.

## Customer-facing patterns

- “Seen it on TikTok? Find it here.”
- “Play the original video here first.”
- “Verified TikTok Shop links appear when the exact product is available.”
- “Watch the video. Find the exact link.”
- “Product links are added once they are verified.”

## Language to avoid

- “I’m adding”, “I have not matched”, “I’m not putting”, or any language about internal review, implementation, or missing build work.
- “Curated”, “editorial story”, “product layer”, “real-time catalogue”, and “shop the edit” as default labels.
- Generic claims such as “good stuff”, “find your thing”, or “worth talking about.”
- Promises that a product works, fits everyone, or produces a body/health result.
- Fake scarcity, countdown language, or unverified social proof.
